Requirements Elicitation and Approval

How we plan elicitation, gather and document requirements, validate them with stakeholders, and secure formal approval.

Back to Library

Purpose

This procedure defines how the BA team turns a framed initiative into clear, testable, agreed-upon requirements. Good requirements are the contract between what stakeholders need and what gets built. Doing this well — documenting plainly, validating openly, and getting an explicit decision — is how we deliver exactly what we sell and finish better friends than when we started.

Scope

Applies to requirements work for any initiative that has cleared intake, across both business units. Covers elicitation planning, the elicitation sessions themselves, documentation, validation, and the formal approval that baselines the requirements.

Out of scope:

  • Intake and problem framing (see Project Discovery and Intake).
  • Changes to requirements after baseline (see Requirements Change Control).
  • Solution design and build decisions owned by delivery teams.

Roles & Responsibilities

RoleResponsibility
Business AnalystPlans and facilitates elicitation, documents requirements, and drives them to approval.
SponsorConfirms scope, resolves conflicting priorities, and provides final approval.
Subject Matter ExpertsSupply process detail, validate accuracy, and confirm feasibility.
End UsersDescribe real-world needs and review requirements for usefulness and clarity.
Product Owner / Delivery LeadReviews requirements for buildability and helps prioritize the backlog.
ApproversNamed decision-makers who formally sign off and baseline the requirements.

Trigger / When to Use

Begin when an initiative has a completed intake summary and a confirmed sponsor, and any of the following is true:

  • Discovery has established a problem worth solving and work is authorized to proceed.
  • A new phase or release needs its requirements defined or refined.
  • A prior effort's requirements are incomplete, ambiguous, or out of date and must be re-established.

Step-by-Step Procedure

1

Plan the elicitation

  • Confirm objectives, stakeholders, and the techniques you'll use (interviews, workshops / JAD, observation, document review).
  • Prepare an interview or workshop guide and schedule sessions with the right people.
2

Elicit the requirements

  • Facilitate sessions to draw out business, functional, and non-functional needs.
  • Capture assumptions, constraints, dependencies, and open questions as you go.
  • Listen for the 'why' behind each ask so requirements stay outcome-driven.
3

Document clearly

  • Record requirements in the agreed format — BRD, user stories with acceptance criteria, or both.
  • Write each requirement so it is clear, testable, and traceable. One need per statement.
  • Log every requirement in the Master Requirements Tracker with a unique ID.
4

Analyze and prioritize

  • Check for conflicts, gaps, and duplicates; run a gap analysis against current state.
  • Prioritize with the sponsor (for example MoSCoW) and confirm what is in and out of scope.
5

Validate with stakeholders

  • Walk stakeholders and SMEs through the documented requirements.
  • Stress-test clarity and testability — the Challenge Requirement Quality prompt helps here.
  • Update the documentation and resolve or log open items.
6

Secure formal approval

  • Present the baseline set to the named approvers.
  • Capture explicit sign-off (who, what, when) and note any conditions.
  • Baseline the approved requirements — from here, changes follow Requirements Change Control.
7

Establish traceability

Link approved requirements into the Requirements Traceability Matrix so they can be tracked through design, build, and testing.

Inputs & Outputs

Inputs

  • Approved intake summary and sponsor
  • Stakeholder and SME availability
  • Existing process maps, systems, and documentation
  • Interview / workshop guides and templates

Outputs

  • Documented requirements (BRD and/or user stories with acceptance criteria)
  • Updated Master Requirements Tracker with unique IDs
  • Recorded stakeholder validation and open-item log
  • Formal approval / baseline and traceability links
