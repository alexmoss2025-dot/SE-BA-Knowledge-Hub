UAT Planning and Execution

How we plan User Acceptance Testing, prepare scenarios, run execution with real users, log defects, and secure sign-off.

Back to Library

Purpose

User Acceptance Testing is where business users confirm that what we built actually meets the approved requirements and works in the real world. This procedure makes UAT organized and fair: coverage is planned, the right users are engaged, defects are captured consistently, and go-live rests on an explicit, documented sign-off — not a hopeful assumption.

Scope

Applies to UAT for any solution or process change delivered through the BA practice, across both business units. Covers UAT planning, test scenario and case preparation, execution, defect management, and final acceptance sign-off.

Out of scope:

  • Unit, integration, and system testing performed by the delivery team before UAT.
  • Requirements definition (see Requirements Elicitation and Approval).
  • Post go-live hypercare and production support activities.

Roles & Responsibilities

RoleResponsibility
Business AnalystPlans UAT, builds scenarios from requirements, coordinates users, and tracks defects to closure.
UAT Testers (End Users)Execute test scenarios, report results honestly, and confirm the solution fits how they work.
Sponsor / Business OwnerConfirms readiness, weighs open defects, and provides final acceptance sign-off.
Delivery / Development TeamFixes defects, deploys to the UAT environment, and confirms resolutions.
Quality TeamOwns this procedure and UAT standards, and reviews entry / exit criteria.

Trigger / When to Use

Begin UAT planning when:

  • Requirements are baselined and the solution has passed system / integration testing.
  • A UAT environment and representative test data are available.
  • A release or go-live date is targeted and business validation is required before launch.

Step-by-Step Procedure

1

Plan UAT

  • Define objectives, scope, entry and exit criteria, timeline, and the UAT environment.
  • Identify and confirm the right business testers and their availability.
  • Agree what 'acceptable' looks like, including defect severity definitions.
2

Prepare scenarios and test cases

  • Derive scenarios from approved requirements and acceptance criteria — cover happy paths, edge cases, and key exceptions.
  • Write each test case with clear steps, test data, and expected results in the UAT Test Script Workbook.
  • Trace scenarios back to requirements so coverage is verifiable. The UAT Scenario Builder can accelerate drafting.
3

Confirm readiness

  • Verify the environment, data, and access are ready and testers are briefed.
  • Hold a short kickoff so testers know how to execute cases and log defects.
4

Execute testing

  • Testers run cases and mark each pass or fail against the expected result.
  • The BA monitors progress, unblocks testers, and keeps daily visibility on coverage and status.
5

Log and triage defects

  • Record each defect with steps to reproduce, severity, and evidence (screenshots).
  • Triage severity with the delivery team and prioritize fixes.
  • Distinguish true defects from change requests — new asks route to Requirements Change Control.
6

Retest and confirm fixes

  • Retest resolved defects and update case status.
  • Track open defects against exit criteria and keep the sponsor informed.
7

Obtain sign-off

  • Review results, coverage, and any remaining defects with the sponsor / business owner.
  • Capture explicit, documented acceptance sign-off (who, what, when) as the basis for go-live.
  • Hand any deferred items to release notes or hypercare with owners and dates.

Inputs & Outputs

Inputs

  • Baselined requirements and acceptance criteria
  • System-tested solution in a UAT environment
  • Representative test data and user access
  • Confirmed UAT testers and schedule

Outputs

  • UAT plan with entry / exit criteria
  • Test scenarios and executed test cases with results
  • Defect log with severities and resolution status
  • Documented acceptance sign-off and deferred-item list
