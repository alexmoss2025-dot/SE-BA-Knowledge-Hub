Requirements Change Control

A lightweight, disciplined way to request, assess, decide, and document changes to already-approved requirements.

Back to Library

Purpose

Once requirements are baselined, changes are normal — but uncontrolled change is how scope creep, blown budgets, and broken trust happen. This procedure keeps change deliberate: every request is logged, its impact is understood before a decision, and the outcome is documented and communicated. It is intentionally lightweight so it protects the work without slowing good ideas down.

Scope

Applies to any proposed change to requirements that have already been approved and baselined under Requirements Elicitation and Approval, for initiatives in analysis, build, or testing.

Out of scope:

  • Refinements made before requirements are baselined (handle within elicitation).
  • Defects — a solution not meeting an approved requirement is a defect, tracked in UAT Planning and Execution, not a change.
  • Minor clarifications with no impact on scope, effort, cost, or timeline (log for traceability, no board review).

Roles & Responsibilities

RoleResponsibility
RequestorRaises the change, explains the business reason, and states the desired outcome.
Business AnalystLogs the request, performs impact analysis, and presents options and a recommendation.
SponsorApproves or rejects changes within their authority and funds approved changes.
Change Control Board (CCB)Reviews changes above the sponsor threshold and decides approve / reject / defer.
Delivery Lead / Product OwnerConfirms effort and re-plans work for approved changes.
PMOOwns this procedure, the change log, and reporting on change volume and trends.

Trigger / When to Use

Start when a change to baselined requirements is proposed, such as:

  • A stakeholder requests new, modified, or removed functionality.
  • A regulatory, policy, or business-priority shift alters what is needed.
  • Discovery during build or testing reveals a required requirement was missed or is wrong.
  • A dependency or constraint changes and forces a scope adjustment.

Step-by-Step Procedure

1

Submit the change request

  • Capture the change on a Change Request (CR): requestor, description, business justification, and desired date.
  • Assign a unique CR ID and log it in the change register.
2

Do a first-pass triage

  • Confirm it is a genuine change to a baselined requirement, not a defect or pre-baseline refinement.
  • Reject or redirect out-of-scope items early, with a clear reason.
3

Perform impact analysis

  • Assess impact on scope, requirements, effort, cost, timeline, risk, and other stakeholders.
  • Identify affected requirements via the Requirements Traceability Matrix.
  • Prepare options where possible (for example: do now, defer to a later phase, or decline) with a recommendation.
4

Route to the right decision level

  • Low-impact changes within authority go to the sponsor.
  • Higher-impact changes go to the Change Control Board.
  • Match the rigor to the size of the change — do not over-govern small asks.
5

Decide

  • The sponsor or CCB decides approve, reject, or defer.
  • Record the decision, rationale, and any conditions on the CR.
6

Update and re-baseline

  • For approved changes, update the requirements, tracker, and traceability matrix, and re-baseline.
  • Re-plan affected work and confirm downstream impacts (design, build, test).
7

Communicate and close

  • Notify the requestor and affected stakeholders of the outcome, whatever it is.
  • Close the CR in the register and keep the audit trail intact.

Inputs & Outputs

Inputs

  • Baselined requirements and traceability matrix
  • Submitted Change Request (CR)
  • Current schedule, budget, and delivery plan
  • Sponsor / CCB decision thresholds

Outputs

  • Logged and triaged CR with impact analysis
  • Documented approve / reject / defer decision and rationale
  • Updated, re-baselined requirements and tracker
  • Stakeholder communication and closed audit trail
