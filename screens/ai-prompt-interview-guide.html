Discovery

Build an Interview Guide

Use this prompt to turn a discovery objective into a focused, well-sequenced stakeholder interview guide. It produces open questions grouped by theme so you can uncover goals, pain points, decisions, and success measures without leading the interviewee.

The Prompt

Copy this into MossAIc, then replace the bracketed placeholders with your context.

You are helping a business analyst prepare for a stakeholder discovery interview.

Context:
- Project / initiative: [describe the project in 1-2 sentences]
- Stakeholder role: [e.g., Operations Manager]
- What I already know: [bullet the key facts you have]
- What I need to learn: [the decisions or gaps this interview must close]

Please produce an interview guide that:
1. Opens with 2 rapport-building questions.
2. Groups 12-15 open-ended questions under these themes: Goals & Success, Current Process & Pain Points, Decisions & Rules, Systems & Data, Risks & Constraints.
3. Avoids leading or yes/no questions.
4. Ends with 2 wrap-up questions (anything missed, who else to talk to).

Format as a clean, printable guide with theme headings and numbered questions.

How to Use It

Follow these steps to get a dependable result.

01

Add your context

Replace every bracketed placeholder with real detail about your project and the stakeholder. The more specific your context, the sharper the questions.

02

Run it in MossAIc

Paste the completed prompt into MossAIc and generate the guide.

03

Review and tailor

Cut questions that don't fit, reorder for flow, and add any organization-specific topics the model wouldn't know.

04

Save to your project

Store the final guide alongside your other discovery artifacts so it's reusable for similar interviews.

Tips for a Better Result

  • Interview one role at a time — run the prompt separately per stakeholder type.
  • Ask MossAIc to 'tighten to 10 questions' if the guide feels long.
  • Follow up with 'suggest probing questions for pain points' during prep.

Before You Prompt

  • Remove confidential or client-identifying details.
  • Give clear context and state the output you need.
  • Review and verify every response before using it.
