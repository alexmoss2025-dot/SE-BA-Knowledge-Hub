Project Discovery and Intake

How a new request becomes a clearly framed, sponsored, and prioritized initiative before analysis work begins.

Back to Library

Purpose

This procedure gives the Moss Business Analysis team one consistent way to receive, evaluate, and log new requests. A disciplined intake protects our partners' time and ours: it confirms there is a real problem worth solving, names an accountable sponsor, and sets a shared understanding of scope before anyone commits hours. Clear is kind — framing the work honestly up front prevents rework, scope creep, and misaligned expectations later.

Scope

Applies to every new request routed to the BA practice — process improvements, system enhancements, reporting needs, and net-new solutions across Construction Management and Solar business units. It covers the moment a request arrives through the go / no-go intake decision and initial logging.

Out of scope:

  • Detailed requirements elicitation (see Requirements Elicitation and Approval).
  • Emergency field safety issues, which follow the site safety escalation path, not BA intake.
  • Pure IT support tickets with no analysis or process change involved.

Trigger / When to Use

Start this procedure when any of the following happens:

  • A stakeholder submits a request through the intake form, email, or a leadership conversation.
  • A recurring pain point surfaces in a retrospective, town hall, or process review.
  • A new system, tool, or regulatory change (for example, IRA compliance) creates a documented need.
  • A leader asks the BA team to assess feasibility of an idea before committing resources.

Step-by-Step Procedure

1

Capture the request

  • Record requestor, date, business unit, and a plain-language description of the ask.
  • Do not reword the problem into a solution yet — capture it in the requestor's own words first.
2

Confirm the problem, not the solution

  • Hold a short discovery conversation to separate the underlying problem from the requested fix.
  • Ask what outcome success would look like and who feels the pain today.
3

Identify the sponsor and stakeholders

  • Name the accountable sponsor who owns the outcome and can fund the work.
  • List primary stakeholders, SMEs, and affected end users. Use the Stakeholder Analysis Matrix if the group is large.
4

Assess value, effort, and risk

  • Estimate business value, rough effort (T-shirt size), urgency, and known risks or dependencies.
  • Flag anything that touches safety, compliance, or in-flight projects.
5

Draft the intake summary

Write a one-page summary: problem statement, desired outcome, sponsor, stakeholders, rough size, and any assumptions or constraints.

6

Make the go / no-go decision

  • Review with the BA Practice Lead and, for larger asks, the business unit leader.
  • Decide: proceed to elicitation, park with a reason, redirect to another team, or decline with a clear rationale.
7

Log and communicate

  • Record the decision and rationale in the intake log and portfolio tracker.
  • Close the loop with the requestor the same week — whatever the answer, they hear it from us directly.

Inputs & Outputs

Inputs

  • Intake request (form, email, or meeting notes)
  • Requestor and sponsor contact details
  • Any existing background docs, tickets, or prior analysis
  • Current portfolio and team capacity view

Outputs

  • Completed one-page intake summary
  • Named sponsor and stakeholder list
  • Documented go / no-go decision and rationale
  • Logged entry in the intake / portfolio tracker
